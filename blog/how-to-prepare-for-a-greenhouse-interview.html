What Greenhouse actually is

Greenhouse is an applicant tracking system — the software thousands of companies use to manage hiring from application to offer. If you've ever applied through a jobs.greenhouse.io link, you've already been inside it.

What sets Greenhouse apart from older ATS platforms is that it's built around structured interviewing. Every candidate for a role gets asked the same questions. Every interviewer scores candidates on the same criteria using a scorecard. The goal is to make hiring decisions based on consistent data rather than gut feel.

That's actually good news for candidates who prepare. Structured processes reward preparation more than charisma.

How the Greenhouse hiring process works

Most Greenhouse pipelines follow the same general shape, even if the number of stages varies by company and seniority level.

Application and resume screening

Your resume enters the system and is reviewed — sometimes by a recruiter manually, sometimes with AI-assisted screening that flags keyword matches against the job requirements. This is the first filter. It never sees you. It only sees text.

Recruiter screen

A 20 to 30 minute call with someone from the talent team. They're checking for basic fit, compensation alignment, and whether you can communicate clearly. They're filling out a Greenhouse scorecard while they talk to you. Your answers to "why this role" and "what are you looking for" land on a form that gets compared to every other candidate they screened.

Hiring manager interview

This is where structured interviewing kicks in fully. The hiring manager is working from a question set built for the role. Behavioral questions dominate — "tell me about a time when..." is the format. They're scoring your answers against specific competencies: strategic thinking, collaboration, execution, communication. Every answer you give maps to a box on their scorecard.

Panel or loop interviews

For mid-level and senior roles, expect a panel of two to four interviewers, each assigned different competency areas. One person owns the behavioral questions. One owns the technical or craft assessment. One might be a peer or cross-functional stakeholder. They each score you independently and compare notes in a debrief afterward.

Final interview and offer

Scorecards from every round are reviewed together. The hiring team looks for consensus. A single strong "no" from one interviewer can block an offer — which is why consistency across your entire panel matters as much as any individual conversation.

What Greenhouse scorecards actually measure

This is the thing most candidates never think about. Every interviewer in a Greenhouse process is evaluating you against a defined set of competencies, not their general impression of you. Those competencies come from the job description.

Common Greenhouse scorecard attributes
Role-specific competencies (from JD) High weight
Communication clarity High weight
Structured thinking and problem solving High weight
Culture and values alignment Medium weight
General energy and presence Low weight

The job description is the scorecard. If a role lists "cross-functional collaboration" as a core requirement, at least one interviewer has been assigned to evaluate exactly that. Prepare a specific story for every major competency in the job description before you go in.

Five things to do before a Greenhouse interview

1. Treat the job description as a question list

Every skill and competency in the JD is a potential scorecard attribute. Go through it and write out the three to five competencies that appear most often or carry the most weight for the role. For each one, prepare a concrete STAR story — Situation, Action, Result — that demonstrates it directly.

2. Research the company's interview format

Greenhouse is the platform, not the process. Every company configures it differently. Check Glassdoor and Levels.fyi for first-person accounts of what the interview loop actually looks like. Former candidates often list the exact questions they were asked. That's your prep list.

3. Prepare for behavioral questions specifically

Greenhouse-powered interviews lean heavily on behavioral questions because that's what structured scorecards are built to evaluate. "Tell me about a time when you had to influence without authority." "Describe a project that didn't go as planned." "Walk me through a decision you made with incomplete information." These are not generic. They map to specific competency boxes. Practice answering them out loud, not in your head.

4. Be consistent across every interviewer

In a panel process, interviewers compare scorecards. If you give a different answer about your "greatest professional accomplishment" to the recruiter than you do to the hiring manager, that inconsistency shows up in the debrief. Lock your core stories and tell them the same way every time.

5. Practice with an AI that scores your answers against the job description

The closest preparation you can do is a realistic voice interview built from your actual job description, scored on answer content and structure. That's what Plumlo does. One session before your Greenhouse loop changes how you show up under pressure.

What to expect on the day

Greenhouse interviews feel more formal than a casual hiring conversation. The interviewer is working through a structured set of questions and taking notes throughout. Don't mistake note-taking for disinterest — it's the process working as designed.

Give complete answers. Don't trail off and ask "does that answer your question?" A structured interviewer is scoring your answer against a rubric. A vague or incomplete answer scores low regardless of how warm the conversation feels.

If you're in a panel loop, treat each conversation as independent. You don't know what the previous interviewer told this one. Cover your core competencies completely in every session.

Common questions

Does Greenhouse make hiring decisions automatically?

No. Greenhouse is a structured hiring platform, not an autonomous AI decision-maker. Every decision is made by humans. The platform organizes scorecards and tracks candidates — it doesn't replace the hiring team's judgment.

What is a Greenhouse scorecard?

A form every interviewer fills out after each interview. It includes numerical ratings for specific competencies and written notes. Scorecards from the full panel are compared to reach a hiring decision.

Why do Greenhouse interviews feel so scripted?

Because they are. Greenhouse is built around structured interviewing — every candidate gets the same questions in the same order. If your interviewer is reading from a list, that's the process working as intended.

How do I know if a company uses Greenhouse?

Check the application URL. If it contains greenhouse.io or boards.greenhouse.io, you're in the system. You may also see a Greenhouse-branded confirmation email after you apply.
