Your Job Interview in English: Questions, Answers and Practice

5 min read  ·  Plumlo

An interview in English tests two things at once: your answers and your English. You don't need perfect grammar. You need answers you have already said out loud. Here are the questions you will almost certainly hear, short sample answers you can adapt, phrases for when you don't understand, and how to practice.

The questions you will almost certainly hear

Most interviews for hourly and entry-level jobs in the US use some version of these. Prepare each one once, out loud, and you have covered most of the interview.

Short sample answers to adapt

Keep the structure and change the details to your own. Simple sentences are easier to say under pressure.

  • Tell me about yourself: "I have three years of experience in customer service, most recently at a grocery store. I'm good with people and I stay calm when it gets busy. I'm looking for a full-time job where I can grow."
  • Why do you want to work here? "I shop here and I like how the team treats customers. I want to work somewhere I can learn and stay for a long time."
  • What are your strengths? "I'm reliable. In my last job I was never late, and my manager asked me to train new people."
  • What is your greatest weakness? "Sometimes I take on too much myself. I'm learning to ask for help sooner, and it makes the team faster."
  • Tell me about a time you solved a problem: "A customer was upset because an order was wrong. I listened, apologized and fixed it right away. She came back the next week and asked for me."
  • Do you have any questions for us? "What does a normal day look like in this job?" or "What do the best people on your team do well?"

What to say when you don't understand

Guessing at a question is worse than asking. These phrases buy you time and sound professional:

  • "Could you repeat the question, please?"
  • "Do you mean my last job or my experience in general?"
  • "Let me think about that for a moment."
  • "Sorry, could you say that a little slower?"

The easy way to structure any answer

For any question that asks for an example, tell it in three parts: what happened, what you did and how it ended. Three sentences is enough. It's a simple version of what recruiters call the STAR method, and it works the same in any language.

Common mistakes

  • Translating in your head word by word. Prepare the answer in English from the start so the sentences come out whole.
  • Memorizing a script. If you forget one word, a script falls apart. Remember the three parts of your story instead.
  • Talking too fast. Nerves speed you up. Slower is clearer.
  • Apologizing for your English. Skip it. Just answer.

Five things to do before your interview

1. Prepare three stories

One about a problem you solved, one about working with a team and one about a time you did more than asked. Most example questions fit one of them.

2. Learn the words from the job posting

Read the posting and write down the words it repeats, like "customer service," "cash handling" or "team player." Use them in your answers.

3. Say every answer out loud

Answers that sound fine in your head often come out differently. Saying them out loud is where you find the words you are missing.

4. Record yourself once

Listen back for speed and for the words you get stuck on. Fix those two things and you will sound much more confident.

5. Do a practice interview in English

Paste the job posting into Plumlo, answer a short practice interview out loud in English, and see exactly what landed and what missed. If you want to sort out your stories first, you can also practice in Spanish and then switch to English.

Common questions

Do I need perfect English to pass a job interview?

No. Interviewers want to understand you and hear clear examples of your work. Short, simple sentences you have practiced out loud beat long sentences you are building in your head. An accent is normal. Being hard to follow is the problem, and practice fixes that.

Can I ask the interviewer to repeat a question?

Yes, and it is better than guessing. Say "Could you repeat the question, please?" or check what they mean: "Do you mean my last job or my experience in general?" Interviewers expect it.

Can I ask to do the interview in Spanish?

You can ask the recruiter before the interview, especially for bilingual roles. Many employers will still want to hear your English if the job uses it, so prepare in English either way.

How long should my answers be?

Aim for about one to two minutes for most questions. For "Tell me about yourself," start with one or two sentences about your work background, then add one example if they want more.

What if I go blank in the middle of an answer?

Pause and say "Let me think about that for a moment." Then go back to your story: what happened, what you did and how it ended. Having three stories ready makes this much easier.
