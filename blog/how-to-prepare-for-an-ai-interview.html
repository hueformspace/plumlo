What an AI interview actually sounds like

AI screening interviews are voice-based, conversational, and built from your job description. They don't sound robotic. The better ones — built on platforms like ElevenLabs or similar — have natural cadence, appropriate pauses, and follow-up prompts that respond to what you say.

You'll typically get five questions. They'll cover your background, your motivation for the role, a behavioral scenario or two, and something specific to the job description. The AI is evaluating clarity, relevance, and structure — not personality.

What catches people off guard isn't the technology. It's the silence. There's no interviewer energy to read, no facial expression to calibrate off of. You answer into a void and the AI moves on.

What AI interviews are actually scoring

Unlike a human interviewer who might give you credit for warmth or enthusiasm, AI screening tools are evaluating the content of your answers against the job description. Specifically:

  • Did you answer what was actually asked, or did you pivot to what you wanted to say?
  • Did your answer include specific evidence — a result, a number, a named situation?
  • Was your answer structured, or did it trail off?
  • Did your experience map to the requirements in the job description?

Generic answers fail AI screening faster than they fail human screening. "I'm a team player who loves challenges" lands nowhere. A specific story about a specific outcome is what gets through.

Five things to do before an AI interview

1. Read the job description like a scoring rubric

The AI's questions are built from it. Every requirement listed is a potential question. Mark the three to five skills that appear most prominently and prepare a specific example for each one.

2. Say your answers out loud before the real thing

Most people have never heard themselves answer interview questions out loud before it counts. Reading an answer in your head and saying it into a microphone are completely different experiences. Practice speaking, not writing.

3. Structure every answer the same way

Situation, action, result. Keep it tight. AI scoring rewards answers that are easy to parse — a clear beginning, a specific action you took, and a concrete outcome. Rambling is penalized the same way it would be on a resume.

4. Don't over-prepare for personality

AI screening isn't evaluating how likable you are. You don't need to be warm or funny or build rapport. You need to be clear, specific, and relevant. That's a different kind of preparation than most people default to.

5. Practice with an AI before you face one
