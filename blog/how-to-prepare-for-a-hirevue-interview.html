What HireVue actually is

HireVue is a one-way video interview platform. There is no recruiter on the other end. You see a question on screen, get 30 seconds to prepare, and record your answer, up to 3 minutes, directly into your camera, according to HireVue's own candidate guide. Your employer's setup may differ. Then it moves to the next question.

Your recordings are submitted automatically when you finish. HireVue doesn't publish a standard length, so check your invite for how many questions there are. The AI scores each response, and the employer decides what happens next.

As of mid-2026, HireVue also runs a separate voice-based product called AI Interviewer, a live, dynamic conversation instead of a pre-recorded one-way video. Which version you get depends entirely on how the employer set up their process, so don't assume it's always the recorded format.

Which companies use HireVue in 2026

HireVue is used heavily in finance, consulting, tech, and retail. If you're applying to any of these organizations, expect to see it:

JPMorgan Goldman Sachs Citi Bain BCG IBM Capital One Microsoft Amazon Deloitte Unilever Delta Air Lines

If you're applying to any company with more than 5,000 employees, assume AI screening is somewhere in the process — whether it's HireVue or something built on the same model.

What HireVue's AI is actually scoring

This is the part most candidates get wrong. They prepare for HireVue like a human interview, practicing rapport, thinking about their energy, trying to seem likable. According to HireVue's own explainability statement, that isn't what the AI scores. HireVue says its AI relies only on what you say, and doesn't use video analysis or other audio characteristics such as tone of voice.

What HireVue says its AI uses
What you say (as a transcript) Scored
Facial expressions, body language Not used
Your background and surroundings Not used
Tone of voice Not used
Filler words and speaking pace Not published

Here is how the scoring works, per the same document. Your speech is converted to text by a third-party transcription system, then each response is scored against behaviorally anchored rating scales for each competency. For employers who use HireVue's modular system, those competencies come from a job analysis done with HireVue's industrial-organizational psychologists. HireVue also says its tool assists employers and the final decision always stays with the employer. On facial analysis, HireVue announced in January 2021 that it was dropping it, as SHRM reported. The practical takeaway is that content is what gets scored: a specific example, a clear structure, and a concrete result.

Why candidates are pushing back on one-way interviews

A Reddit post crossed 31,000 upvotes on r/jobsearchhacks in late September 2026: a candidate who started declining one-way video interviews outright and asking recruiters for a 15-minute phone call instead. About half agreed. The companies that did were, by the poster's account, the only ones who kept respecting their time through the rest of the process — the ones that refused tended to either run six or more interview rounds or go silent after the first one. The poster called the format itself "soul-crushing."

A commenter in that thread cited a field study of more than 3,000 applicants claiming one-way video screens cut application completion by over half, with the steepest drop-off among women and more qualified candidates. That stat hasn't been independently verified, but the reaction it's describing is real and visible in that thread's size alone.

None of that makes HireVue optional for roles at the companies listed above. Refusing it outright isn't realistic if the job is one you actually want. What's driving the backlash is walking into a one-way camera with no sense of pacing, no feedback, and no idea what's being scored. That's the exact gap one practice run closes.

Five things to do before your HireVue

1. Read the job description like a script

HireVue's questions come from the role requirements. Every skill listed is a potential question. Go through the JD and mark the three to five competencies that appear most often. Prepare a specific STAR story for each one before you sit down.

2. Practice out loud — not in your head

Most people prepare by reading notes. HireVue requires you to speak clearly into a camera with no prompting and no human feedback. Those are completely different skills. Speaking your answers once out loud is worth ten times reading them silently.

3. Structure every answer the same way

Situation. Action. Result. Each response is scored against a rating scale for a competency, so an answer with a clear context, a specific action you took, and a concrete outcome gives that scale something to rate. A focused 90-second answer almost always serves you better than a rambling 3-minute one.

4. Trim the filler words

"Um," "like," "you know," "basically" — HireVue's explainability statement says the AI scores what you say, not how you sound, and it doesn't publish anything about filler words, so they aren't a documented scoring factor. They still make an answer harder to follow, and a person may watch your video. You won't notice them when you're nervous. The only way to catch them is to record yourself and listen back before your actual interview.

5. Practice with an AI that scores your answers

The closest preparation you can do is a realistic simulation built from your actual job description, delivered by voice, scored on answer content. That's what Plumlo does. One session before your HireVue changes how you answer under pressure.

A STAR answer that actually scores well

Most prep guides explain the STAR format and stop there. Here's one filled in, built around a competency HireVue screens for constantly: handling a deadline that slipped.

Situation: "Our team was two days from a client launch when the integration we depended on changed its API without notice."

Task: "I was responsible for QA sign-off, and the existing test suite was now failing across the board."

Action: "I triaged the failures by severity instead of fixing them in order, flagged the three that were launch-blocking to the team lead within the hour, and rewrote just those tests while a colleague patched the integration."

Result: "We launched on time. Two of the three blockers were fixed in under four hours, and the triage approach became the team's default for the next dependency break."

Notice what's doing the work: a specific number, a decision explained rather than just stated, and a result that outlasts the one incident. That's what a rubric-based score has to work with, specificity and structure, not how dramatic the story is.

The week before: a day-by-day plan

Prep spread across a week beats a single cram session, because you need to hear yourself say each answer more than once before it sounds natural.

Day 1 — Mark the three to five competencies the job description repeats. That's your question list.

Day 2 — Draft one story per competency, rough bullet points only, not full sentences yet.

Day 3 — Say each story out loud once, unscripted, and time it. Most run long the first try.

Day 4 — Cut each story to 60-90 seconds in STAR order. Say it again until it holds that length without rushing.

Day 5 — Run a full practice interview on questions you haven't pre-written answers for. This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that actually matters.

Day 6 — Fix whatever broke in that practice run. Usually filler words, or an answer that didn't land where you thought it did.

Day 7 — Light review only. Reread the job description once. Don't rehearse new stories the day before. That's where answers start sounding memorized instead of real.

What to expect on the day

You'll receive a link by email. Click it when you're ready — once you start, the clock runs. You can't pause between questions. Some employers allow a retry per question, but assume every take is final.

Test your camera and microphone before you open the link. Find a quiet space with neutral lighting. Don't sit in front of a window — the backlight washes you out. Look at the camera, not the screen. That's where eye contact lives.

HireVue says its AI doesn't use your background. A human reviewer who watches your video may notice it, so keep the space tidy.

Common questions

Does HireVue make the final hiring decision?

No. HireVue says its tool assists employers in evaluating candidates and the final decision always stays with the employer. How the shortlist is built, and whether a recruiter watches every video or only the top-scored ones, depends on the employer, and HireVue doesn't publish that.

Can I retake a HireVue interview?

Some employers allow one retry per question before you submit. Once submitted, the interview is final. Check the instructions inside your HireVue link before starting — the retake policy varies by employer.

How long should my answers be?

Aim for 60 to 90 seconds. HireVue's candidate guide describes up to 3 minutes per answer, but longer isn't better. A focused answer with a clear outcome gives a competency rating scale something concrete to score, and a rambling one buries it.

Is there a way to practice before a real HireVue?

Yes. Plumlo conducts a live voice interview built from your actual job description and scores your answers the way an AI recruiter would. It's the closest simulation to the real thing available.

Does HireVue still use facial recognition?

No. HireVue announced in January 2021 that it was dropping facial analysis, and its 2024 explainability statement says its AI relies only on what the candidate says and doesn't use video analysis or other audio characteristics, including tone of voice. How you look isn't part of the AI score.

Does HireVue score your tone of voice or filler words?

HireVue's 2024 explainability statement says the AI doesn't use tone of voice or other audio characteristics. It scores a transcript of what you say. HireVue doesn't publish anything specific about filler words or speaking pace, so they aren't a documented scoring factor. They can still make an answer harder to follow, and a person may watch your video.

Does HireVue record you?

Yes for the one-way video format. HireVue's candidate guide says your responses are recorded and submitted to the recruiter, lists a webcam as a requirement, and offers practice questions you can repeat before the real interview. HireVue also lists a voice-based AI Interviewer, and which format you get depends on your employer.
