What Tenzo AI actually is

Tenzo AI is a voice-based screening system that runs live, real-time interviews by phone, video, or Zoom, and in some setups by SMS or email first. Unlike a one-way recorded video, you're on an actual call, and the conversation moves based on how you answer, not a fixed list you read into a camera alone.

What sets it apart from most AI screeners is how it handles evaluation. Tenzo uses two separate AI models working together, one runs the conversation, the other scores it against the role's rubric. Every point in your score links back to a specific moment in the transcript, so the evaluation isn't a vague impression, it's tied to something you actually said.

Where you're likely to run into it

Tenzo AI is built to plug into major hiring systems rather than replace them, most commonly Workday, iCIMS, and Greenhouse. If a company runs high-volume hiring on any of those platforms and wants a fast, consistent first-round screen, Tenzo is one of the tools they're likely running underneath it.

It's positioned specifically for teams that need to screen at scale without losing consistency between candidates, so expect it anywhere hiring volume is high and timelines are tight.

What Tenzo's AI is actually scoring

This is where most candidates prepare for the wrong thing. Tenzo isn't listening for charm or energy, it's checking your answers against explicit, role-specific criteria and building an evidence trail a human reviewer can actually audit.

What the AI evaluates
Answer relevance to the specific rubric High weight
Specific, evidenced examples High weight
Consistency of identity through the call High weight
Clarity and structure of response Medium weight
General conversational tone Low weight

Vague, generic answers score poorly here in a very specific way, there's no transcript moment for the system to point to as evidence. Specific answers with a real example attached give the AI something concrete to score.

Five things to do before your Tenzo AI interview

1. Treat the job description as the actual rubric

Tenzo's questions and scoring are built around the role's stated requirements. Go through the job posting and identify the four or five skills or responsibilities that come up most. Have a real, specific example ready for each one before the call starts.

2. Practice answering out loud, in real time

This is a live back-and-forth, not a recording you can redo. The only way to get comfortable thinking on your feet under a real rubric is to practice the actual conversation out loud beforehand, not just review notes silently.

3. Lead every answer with a specific example

Since scoring is tied to evidence in the transcript, a specific story beats a general statement every time. "I handled a difficult customer" scores lower than the actual thirty seconds of what you did and what happened next.

4. Be yourself, literally

Tenzo includes identity verification built to catch someone else taking the call for you. Beyond the obvious ethics of that, it also means the system is designed to notice inconsistency. Answer as yourself, consistently, start to finish.

5. Practice with an AI that scores your answers

The closest preparation available is a live simulation built from your actual job description, delivered by voice, and scored on what you actually say. That's what Plumlo does. One session before your Tenzo screen changes how you answer under pressure.

What to expect on the day

You may get a phone call, a video link, or a Zoom invite, depending on how the employer has it set up, sometimes preceded by a text or email exchange first. Because it's a live conversation, there's no pausing to redo an answer. Find a quiet space with a stable connection before it starts, and treat it exactly like a real interview, because it is one.

Common questions

Does Tenzo AI make the final hiring decision?

No. Tenzo AI runs the first-round screen and writes a scored, timestamped record back into the employer's hiring system. A human recruiter or hiring manager reviews that record and makes the actual decision.

Is a Tenzo AI interview live or recorded?

Live. Tenzo conducts real-time conversations by phone, video, or Zoom, not a one-way recording you submit and walk away from. It can also screen by SMS or email depending on how the employer has it configured.

How is Tenzo AI different from HireVue or Olivia?

HireVue is typically a one-way recorded video. Olivia, from Paradox, mostly handles scheduling and early text-based screening. Tenzo AI conducts a full live voice conversation and scores it against a specific rubric, with every score linked back to the exact moment in the transcript that earned it.

Can I prepare for a Tenzo AI interview?

Yes. Because it's a structured, rubric-based conversation built around the role's actual requirements, preparing against the real job description matters more here than for a generic interview. Practicing the conversation out loud beforehand is the highest-leverage thing you can do.

Does Tenzo AI check if I'm really the one talking?

Yes. Tenzo includes identity verification specifically to catch proxy interviewing, where someone else takes the call for the candidate. Assume the system is built to confirm you are who you say you are, and answer accordingly.
