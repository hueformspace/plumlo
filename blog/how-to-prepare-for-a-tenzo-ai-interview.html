What Tenzo AI actually is

Tenzo AI is a voice-based screening system that runs live, real-time interviews by phone or video, and in some setups with text or email messages around the interview. Unlike a one-way recorded video, you're on an actual call, and the conversation moves based on how you answer, not a fixed list you read into a camera alone.

What sets it apart from most AI screeners is how it handles evaluation. Tenzo describes rubric-based scorecards that the employer configures for each role, with transcript evidence attached to the scores. Tenzo's own terms say its scoring outputs are generated by AI and that the employer, not Tenzo, makes the hiring decision.

Where you're likely to run into it

Tenzo AI is built to plug into hiring systems rather than replace them. Listings for Tenzo mention iCIMS and Workday, and a standalone option. If a company runs high-volume hiring and wants a fast, consistent first-round screen, Tenzo is one of the tools it might use.

It's positioned specifically for teams that need to screen at scale without losing consistency between candidates, so expect it anywhere hiring volume is high and timelines are tight.

What Tenzo says about how it scores you

Tenzo describes rubric-based scoring that the employer configures for each role. It doesn't publish the rubrics or how much each criterion counts, so any guide that gives exact weights is guessing. Here is what Tenzo does say:

What Tenzo says
Answers scored against a rubric the employer sets Stated
Transcript evidence attached to scores Stated, depends on setup
Who makes the hiring decision The employer
Exact weights for each criterion Not published
Conversational tone and energy Not published

The practical takeaway is advice, not a published rule: a specific answer with a real example gives a rubric something concrete to rate, while a vague one gives it nothing to point to.

Five things to do before your Tenzo AI interview

1. Treat the job description as the actual rubric

Tenzo's questions and scoring are built around the role's stated requirements. Go through the job posting and identify the four or five skills or responsibilities that come up most. Have a real, specific example ready for each one before the call starts.

2. Practice answering out loud, in real time

This is a live back-and-forth, not a recording you can redo. The only way to get comfortable thinking on your feet under a real rubric is to practice the actual conversation out loud beforehand, not just review notes silently.

3. Lead every answer with a specific example

Since scoring is tied to evidence in the transcript, a specific story beats a general statement every time. "I handled a difficult customer" scores lower than the actual thirty seconds of what you did and what happened next.

4. Be yourself, literally

Tenzo's candidate terms say identity verification applies when the employer enables it, and Tenzo describes an ID hold-up step. So be ready to confirm who you are, and answer as yourself, consistently, start to finish.

5. Practice with an AI that scores your answers

The closest preparation available is a live simulation built from your actual job description, delivered by voice, and scored on what you actually say. That's what Plumlo does. One session before your Tenzo screen changes how you answer under pressure.

What to expect on the day

You may get a phone call or a video link, depending on how the employer has it set up, sometimes with a text or email exchange around it. Because it's a live conversation, there's no pausing to redo an answer. Find a quiet space with a stable connection before it starts, and treat it exactly like a real interview, because it is one.

Common questions

Does Tenzo AI make the final hiring decision?

No. Tenzo's candidate terms say it provides technology and does not make hiring decisions. The employer evaluates your candidacy and makes all hiring decisions.

Is a Tenzo AI interview live or recorded?

Live. Tenzo runs voice conversations by phone or video, not a one-way recording you submit and walk away from. Employers can also use text and email around the interview, depending on how they set it up. Tenzo's candidate terms say interviews may be recorded when the workflow uses audio or video.

How is Tenzo AI different from HireVue or Olivia?

HireVue is typically a one-way recorded video. Olivia, from Paradox, mostly handles scheduling and early text-based screening. Tenzo AI conducts a full live voice conversation and scores it against a specific rubric, with transcript evidence attached to the scores, depending on how the employer sets it up.

Can I prepare for a Tenzo AI interview?

Yes. Because it's a structured, rubric-based conversation built around the role's actual requirements, preparing against the real job description matters more here than for a generic interview. Practicing the conversation out loud beforehand is the highest-leverage thing you can do.

Does Tenzo AI check if I'm really the one talking?

It can. Tenzo's candidate terms describe identity verification as something the employer can turn on, and Tenzo describes an ID hold-up step and detection of someone else feeding answers. Whether yours uses it depends on the employer, so assume you may be asked to confirm who you are.
